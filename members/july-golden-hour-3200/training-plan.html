This Month

The theme of the month

Golden Hour

July is your Golden Hour. Lighter, brighter and full of momentum. This month is about training that builds shape and confidence, with gym and home options so you can stay consistent right through the summer.

Kassy's note

You do not need a perfect training week. You need a plan you can come back to. Pick the version that fits your day, focus on your form, and keep showing up. That is what builds shape.

This month's focus

Lifted glutes, a smaller-looking waist, open posture and lean, toned definition. Strength training that sculpts and shapes rather than shrinks, so you feel soft, strong and confident.

What to expect

Both gym and home options so you can stay consistent no matter what your week looks like. Lots of glute and core work, posture moves and short, flexible finishers. Follow the gym plan, the home plan, or mix both.

How this training supports your goal

Built around repeatable workouts and progressive overload. Show up, lift a little more each week and stay consistent, and your Golden Hour shape takes care of itself.

Your Plan

How to follow the plan

This is a flexible plan, not a rigid programme. Choose how you want to train each week, focus on your form, and keep showing up. Consistency over perfection.

3 ways to use this plan
1
Structured week
Follow the weekly training planner exactly as written, done for you.
2
Mix & match
Choose gym or home sessions depending on your week. Keep the same focus each day.
3
Busy girl repeat
Choose Glutes A, Shoulders + Arms, Glutes B and repeat them.
🍒

There is no perfect way to follow the plan. The best version is the one you can repeat consistently.

Simple rules
  • Prioritise both glute days if shape is your goal.
  • Choose gym, home or both.
  • Log your main lifts and aim to progress slowly.
  • Keep upper body controlled, no trap-dominant form.
  • Add finishers only when recovered.
  • Rest when your body needs it.
Smart training

Use progressive overload where possible: weight, reps, tempo, range, control or band tension.

Small changes like these keep workouts effective without overcomplicating it.

Make It Yours · Part 2

How to follow your tier

Same eight workouts for everyone. Your tier sets where you stop, what you protect and what you add only when recovery is good.

Tier 1 · Lean
Fat Loss
Minimum Effective Plan
Fat Loss stops here
Non-negotiables
3 sessions per week minimum, 8–10k steps most days, follow the matching Lean meal tier.
How to follow the workouts
Complete the warm-up, main workout and core if included. Stop there if energy, time or recovery are limited.
Weekly structure
Glutes A, Shoulders + Arms, Glutes B. Pull + Posture only as an optional 4th session when time, energy and recovery are good.
Effort
Main lifts still need to feel challenging, with 1–2 reps in reserve. Strength holding steady is a win in a deficit.
Skip first
Finishers, add-ons and extra intense cardio. Keep steps and main sessions first.
On track if
Steps are hit 5+ days per week, 3 sessions are done and strength is holding steady.
Off track if
You are chasing burn over load, skipping meals to speed things up or restarting every Monday.
Tier 2 · Fuel
High Activity
Full Plan
Full session ends here
Non-negotiables
Full 4-day plan where possible, eat to the Fuel meal tier, 7+ hours of sleep most nights.
How to follow the workouts
Complete the warm-up, main workout, core if included and the finisher when recovered. Add-ons only on good-energy days.
Weekly structure
Follow the 4-day plan as written. Do not add random extra workouts outside the plan.
Effort
Progress one variable per week where possible: weight, reps, tempo, control or range on main lifts.
Skip first
Extra workouts outside the plan. If recovery drops, remove extras before removing rest.
On track if
Energy is stable across the week, sessions feel strong and you recover between days.
Off track if
You are dragging through sessions, sleep is slipping or you keep adding volume just because you feel fine.
Tier 3 · Build
Glute Growth
Priority Glute Plan
Glute Growth ends here
Non-negotiables
Glutes A and Glutes B every week, Build meal tier, log the first 2–3 lifts of every glute session.
How to follow the workouts
On glute days, complete the full session and add the Glute Growth Bonus when recovered. Upper-body days are support work, so do not add volume that affects glute recovery.
Priority order, not a schedule
1. Glutes A · 2. Glutes B · 3. Pull + Posture · 4. Shoulders + Arms. Keep at least one easier day between glute days. Busy 3-day week: Glutes A, one upper-body day, Glutes B.
Effort
One measurable improvement per week on a tracked glute lift: +1 rep, a small load jump, slower lower, deeper range or stronger band tension.
Skip first
Add-ons and upper-body support work before glute days. Glute days stay the priority. Like-for-like glute swaps only.
On track if
Both glute days are done across the month and at least one tracked glute lift improves from Week 1 to Week 4.
Off track if
Glute lifts are flat for 3+ weeks, glute days become cardio, or you add volume before checking food, sleep, form and recovery.

Get Stronger Each Week

Progression guide

Shape is built by repeating the workouts and slowly doing a little more. Here is how the month builds, week by week.

Week 1

Learn the workouts, choose your starting weights and focus on form.

Week 2

Add reps, improve control or reduce rest slightly.

Week 3

Add weight, band tension, range of motion or harder variations.

Week 4

Push intensity while keeping form clean. Aim to beat Week 1.

The rule

If you hit the top of the rep range with good form, increase the weight slightly next time.

How hard sets should feel

Leave 1–2 reps in the tank on main lifts. The last 2–3 reps should feel challenging but controlled, never sloppy.

When to increase weight

When you hit the top of the rep range for all sets with clean form, add a small amount of load or band tension next session.

When to rest more

If form breaks down, you feel run down, or sleep and recovery are poor, take an extra rest day. Recovery is when shape is built.

When to use the busy girl option

Travel, low energy or a packed week? Drop to the 3-day busy week or a 30-minute session. Showing up beats skipping.

Train Smarter

Form cue library

Good form is what turns reps into results. Keep these cues in mind on every exercise.

Glute cues

Push through your heels, squeeze at the top, control the lower, keep your ribs down and avoid rushing reps.

Upper body cues

Shoulders down, neck relaxed, no swinging, slow lower and control the weight throughout.

Back / posture cues

Pull your elbows down and back, keep your chest lifted, avoid shrugging and squeeze your shoulder blades.

Core cues

Ribs down, lower back controlled, move slowly, exhale as you crunch and avoid pulling from your neck.

Tempo + control

Slow reps make lighter weights harder. Use pauses, pulses and controlled lowers to make every movement more effective.

Progressive overload

Add weight, reps, sets, band tension, range of motion, slower tempo or better control over time. Any of these is progress.

Glute Growth cues

Pause at the top, control the lower, use full range and track the key glute movements every week. If you cannot feel the glute working, slow the rep down before adding more weight.

Small waist core cues

Focus on control, breathing and ribs down. Avoid turning every ab exercise into a twisting movement.
