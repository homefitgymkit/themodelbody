The theme of the month

Peach Season

August is Peach Season. Peak summer, peak glutes and the confidence that comes with both. This month is about training that builds real shape, with gym and home options so you stay consistent through the busiest, sunniest weeks of the year.

This month's focus

Glutes first, every week. Two dedicated glute days built on heavy hip thrusts and Romanian deadlifts, plus a push day and a pull day that build the shoulders and back that make your waist look smaller. Core work in every single session.

What to expect

Four workouts, each with a gym and a home version, so a packed August week never breaks your streak. Every session finishes with a ten-minute core block and a burnout finisher. Follow the gym plan, the home plan, or mix the two.

How this training supports your goal

The same four sessions repeated all month, so you can actually track what you lift. The ten-set hip thrust is your benchmark. Beat week one by week four and your Peach Season shape takes care of itself.

How to follow the plan

Same eight workouts for everyone. Your goal sets where you stop, what you protect and what you add only when recovery is good.

Fat Loss Foundation
3–4 workouts a week
Fat Loss ends here
Time25–35 minSets3–4Reps8–15IntensityModerate
Workouts per week
3–4 workouts. Three is the minimum that works; a fourth is a bonus when time and energy allow.
How to follow the workouts
Complete the warm-up, main workout and core if included. Stop there if energy, time or recovery are limited.
If you need a shorter week
Drop to three workouts. One session can be skipped without losing progress. Keep your steps up on the day you skip.
Effort
Main lifts still need to feel challenging, with 1–2 reps in reserve. Strength holding steady is a win in a deficit.
Recovery & consistency
Protect sleep, keep steps high and come back to the plan each week. Consistency beats intensity here.
On track if
Three sessions are done most weeks, steps are hit 5+ days and strength is holding steady.
Off track if
You are chasing burn over load, skipping meals to speed things up or restarting every Monday.

Workouts per week
4 workouts. Follow the full four-day plan as written.
How to follow the workouts
Complete the warm-up, main workout, core if included and the finisher when recovered.

Drop to three workouts by skipping Pull Day. Keep both glute days and one upper-body day in.

Progress one variable per week where possible: weight, reps, tempo, control or range on main lifts.

7+ hours of sleep most nights and a rest day between hard sessions. Recover well before adding anything extra.

4–5 workouts a week
Glute Growth ends here
Time50–65 minSets4–5Reps8–15IntensityHigh
Workouts per week
4–5 workouts. Four is the standard week; add a third glute day to reach five when recovery is good.
How to follow the workouts
On glute days, complete the full session through the sculpt finisher. Upper-body days are support work, so do not add volume that affects glute recovery.
If you need a shorter week
Drop to four days by skipping the upper-body day. Keep both glute days, they are the priority.
Effort
One measurable improvement per week on a tracked glute lift: +1 rep, a small load jump, slower lower, deeper range or stronger band tension.
Recovery & consistency
Keep at least one easier day between glute days, sleep 7+ hours and progress over time. Recovery is what lets the glutes grow.
On track if
Both glute days are done across the month and at least one tracked glute lift improves from Week 1 to Week 4.
Off track if
Glute lifts are flat for 3+ weeks, glute days become cardio, or you add volume before checking food, sleep, form and recovery.

Train Smarter Each Week

Progression, swaps & form

How the month builds week by week, how to swap any move and the cues that keep your form clean.

Week 1

Learn the workouts, choose your starting weights and focus on form.

Week 2

Add reps, improve control or reduce rest slightly.

Week 3

Add weight, band tension, range of motion or harder variations.

Week 4

Push intensity while keeping form clean. Aim to beat Week 1.

The rule

Tempo and control: slow reps make light weights harder, so use pauses, pulses and controlled lowers. Progressive overload: add weight, reps, sets, band tension, range, tempo or control over time. Any of these is progress.

How hard sets should feel

Leave 1–2 reps in the tank on main lifts. The last 2–3 reps should feel challenging but controlled, never sloppy.

When to increase weight

When you hit the top of the rep range for all sets with clean form, add a small amount of load or band tension next session.

When to rest more

If form breaks down, you feel run down, or sleep and recovery are poor, take an extra rest day. Recovery is when shape is built.

Glute
Swaps

Hip thrust→glute bridge / dumbbell bridge / banded bridge
Cable kickback→banded / ankle-weight kickback
Hip abduction machine→banded / side-lying abductions
Romanian deadlift→dumbbell / single-leg RDL

Form

Push through your heels, squeeze at the top, control the lower and keep your ribs down. Use full range, pause at the top and track your key glute lifts. If you cannot feel the glute working, slow the rep down before adding weight.

Upper Body
Swaps

Machine shoulder press→dumbbell / seated / band press
Cable lateral raise→dumbbell / bottle raise
Rear delt machine→rear delt fly / band pull-apart
Tricep pushdown→band pushdown / overhead extension

Form

Shoulders down, neck relaxed and no swinging. Control the weight and lower slowly on every rep.

Back + Posture
Swaps

Lat pulldown→banded / straight-arm band pulldown
Chest-supported row→dumbbell row / band row
Face pull→band face pull / pull-apart

Form

Pull your elbows down and back, keep your chest lifted, avoid shrugging and squeeze your shoulder blades.

Core
Swaps

Plank→incline plank
Leg raises→reverse crunches
Toe touches→crunches
Dead bugs→heel taps

Form

Ribs down, lower back controlled and move slowly. Exhale as you crunch and avoid pulling from your neck. Keep your control and breathing steady rather than twisting every rep.

Your Club

Keep going

Whatever your goal, the rule is the same: come back to the plan. Fat Loss Foundation is the essential sessions done consistently, Balanced Toning is the full plan with proper recovery, and Glute Growth is protecting both glute days and progressing over time. You do not need a perfect week. You need repeatable workouts, small progress and enough consistency to keep going.

🍒
Repeat the workouts
The sessions you come back to are the ones that build shape. Lean on them, do not chase novelty.
🍒
Track your small wins
One more rep, a little more weight, one more session than last week. That is how progress is made.
🍒
Pair it with your meals
Following the matching meal plan tier? Pair the two for the best results. If not, the training stands on its own.
🍒
Come back next month
A fresh plan lands every month, new theme, same simple gym and home approach.

Build the routine that feels good enough to keep. Consistency, not perfection, is what changes your body.
