This month

The Comfort Collection

November is your reminder that progress does not have to feel punishing. This month brings together focused training, warming meals and small routines you can come back to when the days feel darker and busier.

Do the basics beautifully

Choose meals you look forward to, train with control and let your routine grow around real life. You do not need a perfect month; you need a rhythm you can repeat.

A focused training month

Two glute days, one arms-and-shoulders day and one pull day. Keep the same workout number when switching between gym and home.

Complete the Main Workout for your level. Choose gym or home, not both. See Your Training Level for sets and realistic session timings.

Set up for a good session

Equipment guide

Gym

  • Hip-thrust/glute-drive or Smith machine
  • Dumbbells and a stable bench
  • Cable station and suitable attachments
  • Hyperextension station
  • Leg curl and hip abductor
  • Lat pulldown, row and pull-up station

Home

  • One or two dumbbells
  • Long resistance band and mini band
  • Stable sofa or bench for hip thrusts
  • Mat and a clear floor space
  • Towels or sliders for hamstring curls
  • Secure band anchor suitable for the exercise

Flexible alternatives

  • No overhead band anchor: use a dumbbell pullover for pulldowns.
  • No seated-row anchor: use a supported dumbbell row.
  • No sliders: use a bridge walkout with short controlled steps.
  • No load heavy enough: slow the lowering and pause before adding more sets.
Inspect bands and secure anchors before use. Furniture must be stable. Home 2 uses a close-grip floor press as the alternative to chair dips; Home 3 uses quadruped hip extension instead of balancing on a sofa.

Small details, better sessions

Kassy’s monthly picks

The hip-thrust lockout

Finish with a squeeze and stacked ribs. Hold briefly, then lower with control.

The 1¼ rep

A full lift, a quarter lower, another lift, then all the way down. One controlled sequence counts as one rep.

The three kickback angles

Diagonal, low/back and side target slightly different movement directions. Choose at most ONE optional move for 1–2 sets; beginners omit optional work.

The quiet shoulder raise

Raise only as high as you can without shrugging. Lighter weights can make this harder in the right way.

The back-day elbow cue

Think elbows towards your ribs or hips. Keep the body still instead of using momentum.

The home-session win

A well-executed home session keeps the same training rhythm. Log the home variation separately from gym loads.

Your best session is the one you can perform well and recover from. Optional work is a choice, never a test of commitment.

Build through the month

Progression

Week 1 / Find your baseline

Choose loads you can control. Record the variation, weight, reps and how the final set felt. Start with less optional work.

Week 2 / Own the movement

Repeat the setup and range. Add a rep only if the same control is there. Keep one to three good reps in reserve on most sets.

Week 3 / Progress one thing

Once you reach the top of the rep range on every set, use the smallest sensible load increase. For fixed tens, add load only when all tens are clean.

Week 4 / Consolidate

Compare with Week 1. Better technique counts. If fatigue is rising, reduce accessory sets rather than forcing a heavier week.

Keep the movement pattern

Glutes: swaps & form

Useful swaps

  • Hip-thrust machine → Smith, barbell or dumbbell hip thrust.
  • Glute drive → dumbbell hip thrust using the same 1¼-rep pattern.
  • Hyperextension → good morning or hip hinge.
  • Bulgarian RDL → supported single-leg RDL; use a small range first.
  • Cable kickback → band kickback or controlled bodyweight hip extension.
  • Leg curl → slider curl or bridge walkout.

Form anchors

  • Hip thrust: chin gently tucked, ribs down, pelvis controlled.
  • RDL: soft knees, hips back and load close to the legs.
  • Hyperextension: hinge at the hips; avoid throwing the lower back into extension.
  • Kickback: keep the torso still and use a small controlled range.
  • Abduction: move from the hips without rocking the whole body.
Kickback directions: diagonal travels back and slightly out; low travels back with a small controlled lift; side travels away from the midline. Use a controlled range and a familiar setup.

Stay controlled through every rep

Upper body & core: form

Upper-body swaps

  • Smith press → dumbbell press.
  • Chest press → floor press or incline push-up.
  • Pulldown → secure band pulldown or dumbbell pullover.
  • Cable row → supported dumbbell row.
  • Pushdown → overhead dumbbell extension.
  • Dead hang → supported lat stretch.

Upper-body cues

  • Keep shoulders relaxed on raises.
  • Press with steady ribs; avoid leaning back.
  • Row with your elbow, not a body swing.
  • Use a pain-free range on dips or choose a press.
  • Stop hanging if shoulders or grip feel uncomfortable.

Core control

  • Brace before the repetition and keep breathing.
  • Choose a range that lets your lower back stay supported.
  • For planks, shorten the hold or place knees down if the hips sag.
  • For dead bugs and hollow holds, bend the knees before adding load.
  • Keep reverse crunches unweighted until controlled; use no loose weight held between the feet.
Warm up before each session. Stop for sharp pain, dizziness or unusual symptoms and choose an appropriate alternative.

Glutes 1

Growth, lift & roundness

Switch to the home alternative →

Warm-up

5 minutes of easy movement, then a light glute-activation drill. Try one easy set of 15-20 banded bridge abductions; keep it easy enough to warm up without tiring the main lifts.

Complete Workout

Glute Activation Warm-Up 3 × 30
Hip Thrust Machine 4 × 10
B-Stance Hip Thrust 3 × 10
Weighted Hyperextension 3 × 10
Single-Leg Hyperextension 3 × 10 / leg
Bodyweight Hyperextension 3 × 10
Bulgarian RDL 2 × 10 / leg
Sumo Dumbbell Squat 3 × 10
Cable Degree Kickback 2 × 10 / leg
Cable Floor Kickback 2 × 10 / leg
Cable Side Kickback 2 × 10 / leg
Seated Leg Curl 3 × 10

Session focus

Controlled repetitions, progressive overload and a deliberate glute squeeze at the top of each movement.

10-minute Cable abs

Three rounds. Rest 20 seconds between exercises and 30 seconds between rounds. Aim for about 10 minutes; quality takes priority over the clock.

  1. Kneeling Cable Crunch - 15 reps
  2. Standing Cable Crunch - 12 reps
  3. Cable Reverse Crunch - 12 reps
  4. Kneeling Crunch Pulses - 15 reps

Use secure ankle cuffs for cable reverse crunches; if setup is uncertain, use the unweighted lying version.

Form focus

Keep ribs stacked above your pelvis. Finish hip thrusts by extending the hips, not arching the back.

Your workload

Beginner: two sets per main move, no optional work. Intermediate: printed sets; one optional move at most. See Your Training Level.

Heavy lifts: rest 2-3 min.
Isolation: rest 60-90 sec.
Keep 1-3 good reps in reserve.

Make room to recover

Recovery & stretch

Allow ten to twelve easy minutes after training, or use this sequence on a rest day. Stretch gently and stay within a comfortable range.

Figure-four glute stretch 45-60 seconds each side
Half-kneeling hip-flexor stretch 45 seconds each side
Hamstring hinge stretch 45 seconds each side
Child’s pose with lat reach 45 seconds each side
Doorway chest stretch 30-45 seconds each side
Thread the needle 6 slow repetitions each side
Cat-cow 8 slow repetitions
Slow, relaxed breathing 5 breaths
Keep easy days easy. Adequate food, sleep and space between hard sessions support the work you do in the gym.

Choose the dose you can recover from

Your training level

Beginner / build your base

Use two working sets per main movement, omit optional work, and start with abs on Monday and Thursday only. Keep 2-3 good repetitions in reserve. Use straight hip-thrust reps before progressing to 1¼ reps. If four sessions feel too much, use the three-day option in the weekly planner.

Intermediate / the printed prescription

Follow the Main Workout sets. Add at most ONE optional move for 1-2 sets when recovery and time are good. Keep 1-3 good reps in reserve. The glute days now contain seven targeted main sets each; do not stack the gym and home alternatives.

Allow realistic time

Session Beginner Intermediate
Glutes 1 40-55 min 45-60 min
Arms & shoulders 30-45 min 50-65 min
Glutes 2 40-55 min 45-60 min
Pull day 30-45 min 50-65 min

Estimates include a 5-8 minute warm-up and scheduled abs. Optional work adds about 5-10 minutes; the full stretch sequence adds another 10-12. Equipment queues and unilateral work can take longer.

Reduce sets if soreness changes your movement or performance repeatedly falls. Stop for pain or unusual symptoms. This is a general programme, not an individual assessment.

Programming reference: ACSM 2026 resistance-training guidance. The source split is retained with a reduced, practical working-set prescription.

A rhythm worth carrying forward

Keep going

A strong month is made by returning. One busy day does not need to become a lost week. Keep the next choice small: your next meal, your next walk, your next set, your next bedtime. That is the rhythm you can carry forward.

Look back at your month

  • One lower-body lift: more load, more reps or better control.
  • One upper-body lift: a steadier setup and cleaner repetitions.
  • One home session that helped you keep the routine.
  • One core movement that feels more controlled.

Keep the next step small
