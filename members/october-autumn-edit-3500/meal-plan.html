The Autumn Edit

October is the shift into proper autumn food: warm breakfasts, crisp seasonal salads, cosy bowls and comfort dinners that still feel light. The Autumn Edit keeps the Model Body rules — protein first, dairy-free, oil-free and deliberately low-salt — without making the month feel restrictive.

This Month's Focus

Make healthy eating feel easy enough to repeat. Build each meal around protein, use herbs, citrus, spices and naturally sweet autumn produce for flavour, and let the recipes do the planning for you.

What To Expect

Pumpkin, pear, apple, plum, roasted squash, warming spices, colourful salads, noodle bowls, orzo, cottage pie, soup and high-protein puddings. Cosy where it should be, fresh where it needs to be.

How These Meals Support Your Goal

Every recipe has a clear protein anchor and sensible portions. Use the recipes as written for the standard plan, reduce carbs or fats before protein if you need less food, or add extra carbs around training if you are supporting glute growth.

Kassy’s meal note

October is not about eating perfectly. Pick the recipes you genuinely look forward to, keep protein high and make your week easy before you get hungry. The more repeatable it feels, the better it works.

Your plan

How to follow + choose your goal

Same 28 recipes. Choose your portions around your goal, hunger and activity.

Fat Loss Foundation

Approx. 1,500–1,700 kcal per day.
Approx. 100–130g protein per day.

Use the recipes as written, keep portions moderate and repeat the meals that make consistency easiest. If you need less food, reduce a carb or fat portion slightly before reducing protein.

Balanced Toning

Approx. 1,700–1,900 kcal per day.
Approx. 110–140g protein per day.

Use one breakfast, lunch, dinner and snack as your base day. Adjust around hunger and activity. Add fruit, a slightly larger carb portion or another snack if training demands it.

Glute Growth

Approx. 1,900–2,200 kcal per day.
Approx. 120–160g protein per day.

Keep protein high and increase carbs around training. Add extra rice, potato, pasta, oats, fruit or another snack rather than changing the protein anchor.

Simple rules
  • Prioritise protein at every meal.
  • Use a non-stick pan, air fryer, oven, water sautéing or baking paper instead of added cooking oil.
  • Keep added salt minimal; use citrus, herbs, chilli, spices, vinegar, garlic and ginger for flavour.
  • Add vegetables freely.
  • Stay hydrated.
  • Repeat favourites when busy.
Cooking notes

Preheat ovens and air fryers as directed by your appliance. Timings are estimates; allow extra time if needed. Meat and fish weights are raw unless stated otherwise. Use the cooked endpoint in each recipe.

🍒

Macros are estimates, not individually verified values. Weigh bread, wraps, fruit and dumplings, and log your chosen brands and optional extras if tracking. Use cooked or dry weights as written.

Make it work for you

Adapt & swap

The golden rule

Keep the protein anchor, then swap like for like. If a swap lowers protein, increase the portion or add another protein source.

Animal Protein

Chicken ↔ lean turkey ↔ prawns ↔ white fish ↔ salmon ↔ lean beef. Adjust fats where needed.

Plant Protein

Tofu ↔ tempeh ↔ seitan ↔ edamame ↔ lentils ↔ chickpeas. Choose lower-salt versions where possible.

Eggs

Whole eggs can be partly swapped for egg whites when you want more protein with less fat.

Dairy-Free

Milk: unsweetened soy, almond or oat.

Yoghurt: high-protein dairy-free yoghurt.

Protein powder: dairy-free plant-based vanilla or chocolate; check the label.

Keep all October recipes dairy-free as written.

Carbs

Rice, quinoa, bulgur, pasta, orzo, noodles, potatoes, wraps, sourdough and oats can be swapped like for like by sensible portion.

Fats

Avocado, seeds, dark chocolate and salmon provide fats in the plan. Keep portions measured rather than adding oil.

Low-Salt Swaps

Choose no-added-salt tomatoes and stock, reduced-sodium soy sauce, lower-sodium dumplings and fresh/frozen prawns rather than brined options.

Prep once

Cinnamon Apple Protein Oats

Why you’ll love it

A cosy weekday breakfast that still gives you a strong protein hit.

Ingredients
  • 45g oats
  • 25g vanilla dairy-free plant protein powder
  • 200ml unsweetened soy milk
  • 1 small apple, diced
  • 50g blueberries
  • 1 tsp maple syrup
  • 1 tsp cinnamon
  • 1/2 tsp vanilla extract
Method
  1. Add the oats, soy milk, half the apple and half the cinnamon to a saucepan.
  2. Cook over a medium-low heat for 5-7 minutes, stirring, until thick and creamy. Add a splash of water if needed.
  3. Remove from the heat and stir through the protein powder and vanilla until smooth.
  4. Top with the remaining apple, blueberries, maple syrup and cinnamon.
Tips & notes

Use soy milk for the highest-protein dairy-free version. Add a splash of water if your protein powder thickens quickly.

Why you’ll love it

A different warm breakfast base from oats, with plenty of fibre and protein.

Ingredients
  • 55g dry quinoa, rinsed
  • 180ml unsweetened soy milk
  • 1 small pear, diced
  • 20g vanilla dairy-free plant protein powder
  • 1 tsp maple syrup
  • 1 tsp cinnamon
  • 1/2 tsp vanilla extract

Method
  1. Put the rinsed quinoa, soy milk, 80ml water, half the pear and half the cinnamon in a small saucepan.

  4. Top with the remaining pear, maple syrup and cinnamon.
Tips & notes

Rinse quinoa well before cooking to keep the flavour clean and mild.

Why you’ll love it

A high-protein savoury breakfast that feels like brunch without being complicated.

Ingredients
  • 150g no-added-salt passata or chopped tomatoes
  • 80g red pepper, diced
  • 30g onion, finely diced
  • 1 garlic clove, crushed
  • 1 egg
  • 120g egg whites
  • Large handful spinach
  • 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1/2 tsp ground cumin
  • Chilli flakes, to taste
  • Black pepper
  • 1 thick slice sourdough
  • Fresh parsley or coriander
Method
  1. Add the onion and pepper to a non-stick pan with a splash of water and cook for 4-5 minutes.
  2. Add the garlic, smoked paprika, cumin and chilli flakes, then stir in the passata.
  3. Simmer for 5 minutes and stir through the spinach.
  4. Pour in the egg whites, make a small well and crack in the whole egg.
  5. Cover and cook gently for about 7-10 minutes, until the egg whites and yolk are set. Add a splash of water if the sauce becomes too thick.
  6. Toast the sourdough and serve alongside, finished with herbs and black pepper.
Tips & notes

Keep the heat gentle after adding the eggs so the whites set without the sauce catching.

Why you’ll love it

Quick enough for a weekday but more seasonal than a standard cereal bowl.

Ingredients
  • 2 wheat biscuits
  • 150ml unsweetened soy milk
  • 20g vanilla dairy-free plant protein powder
  • 100g high-protein dairy-free yoghurt
  • 1 plum, diced
  • 1 tsp maple syrup
  • 1/2 tsp cinnamon
  • 1/2 tsp vanilla extract
Method
  1. Add the plum to a small pan with a splash of water and half the cinnamon. Cook for 3-4 minutes until soft and glossy.
  2. Crumble the wheat biscuits into a bowl and pour over the soy milk.
  3. Mix the yoghurt, protein powder and vanilla until smooth and spoon over the softened wheat biscuits.
  4. Top with the warm plum, maple syrup and remaining cinnamon.
  5. Eat straight away or chill overnight for a firmer texture.
Tips & notes

Make it the night before if you prefer the firmer overnight-Weetabix texture.

Why you’ll love it

A proper café-style breakfast that still follows the October rules.

Ingredients
  • 2 eggs
  • 120g high-protein dairy-free yoghurt
  • 80g red pepper, sliced
  • Large handful spinach
  • 1 thick slice sourdough
  • Juice of 1/4 lemon
  • 1/4 tsp garlic powder
  • 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
  • Chilli flakes, to taste
  • Fresh dill or parsley
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Heat the oven to 200°C / 180°C fan. Spread the pepper on a lined tray and roast for about 20-25 minutes, turning halfway, until soft and lightly coloured.
  2. Mix the yoghurt with the lemon juice, garlic powder, chopped dill and black pepper.
  3. Wilt the spinach in a non-stick pan with a splash of water.
  4. While the pepper roasts, poach the eggs in gently simmering water for about 4-6 minutes, until the whites and yolks are set; cook longer if needed.
  5. Spoon the yoghurt into a bowl and top with the spinach, roasted pepper and eggs.
  6. Finish with smoked paprika, chilli flakes and herbs.
  7. Toast the sourdough, cut into soldiers and serve alongside.
Tips & notes

Season with herbs, paprika, chilli and lemon before reaching for extra salt.

Why you’ll love it

The most October breakfast in the plan, but still balanced enough for a normal morning.

Ingredients
  • 40g oat flour
  • 20g vanilla dairy-free plant protein powder
  • 1 egg
  • 80g pumpkin purée
  • 60ml unsweetened soy milk
  • 1/2 tsp baking powder
  • 1/2 tsp cinnamon
  • Pinch of ground ginger
  • Pinch of nutmeg
  • 1 tsp maple syrup
  • 60g high-protein dairy-free yoghurt

Method

Tips & notes

Use a genuinely non-stick waffle maker; the batter contains no added oil.

Why you’ll love it

A satisfying Sunday breakfast with protein, fibre and proper brunch energy.

Ingredients
  • 160g baby potatoes, diced
  • 2 eggs
  • 75g egg whites
  • 50g black beans, drained and rinsed
  • 80g red pepper, diced
  • 60g cherry tomatoes, halved
  • Large handful spinach
  • 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
  • 1/2 tsp ground cumin
  • Juice of 1/4 lime
  • Fresh coriander or parsley
  • Black pepper
Method
  1. Cut the potatoes into 1.5cm pieces. Air-fry at 190°C for about 15-20 minutes, shaking halfway, until tender inside and crisp at the edges.
  2. Add the red pepper and tomatoes to a non-stick pan with a splash of water and cook until softened.
  3. Add the spinach, black beans, smoked paprika, cumin and crispy potatoes.
  4. Scramble the eggs and egg whites together in a non-stick pan over a medium-low heat, stirring until fully set.
  5. Serve over the hash and finish with lime juice, herbs and black pepper.
Tips & notes

Air-fry the potatoes first, then fold them into the veg at the end to keep the edges crisp.

High ProteinDairy freeOil FreeFresh
Why you’ll love it

It feels fresh and crunchy while the pear keeps it properly autumnal.

Ingredients
  • 130g chicken breast
  • 1 small pear, thinly sliced
  • 80g red cabbage, finely shredded
  • 60g cucumber, sliced
  • 40g celery, thinly sliced
  • 40g rocket or mixed leaves
  • Dressing
  • 60g high-protein dairy-free yoghurt
  • 1 tsp Dijon mustard
  • Juice of 1/2 lemon
  • 1 tsp maple syrup
  • 1/2 tsp dried thyme
  • Black pepper
  • 1-2 tbsp water, as needed
Method
  1. Season the chicken with thyme and black pepper. Cut the chicken into 1.5cm-thick strips. Cook in a non-stick pan over medium-high heat for about 8-12 minutes, turning often and adding a splash of water if it catches. Check the thickest part reaches 75°C for 30 seconds; cook longer if needed.
  2. Mix the yoghurt, Dijon, lemon juice, maple syrup and enough water to make a spoonable dressing.
  3. Toss the cabbage, cucumber, celery, rocket and pear with half the dressing.
  4. Slice the chicken over the salad and finish with the remaining dressing and black pepper.
Tips & notes

Keep the dressing separate until serving if you are meal-prepping.

Why you’ll love it

Creamy without dairy or oil, with a completely different flavour profile from the summer pasta recipes.

Ingredients
  • 120g chicken breast
  • 65g dry pasta
  • 80g fresh red pepper, sliced
  • 60g cucumber, diced
  • 50g peas, cooked and cooled
  • Large handful spinach
  • Roasted Pepper Dressing
  • 60g high-protein dairy-free yoghurt
  • Half the roasted pepper prepared above
  • 1 tbsp lemon juice
  • Large handful fresh basil
  • 1/2 garlic clove
  • 1/2 tsp smoked paprika
  • Black pepper
  • 1-2 tbsp water
Method
  1. Cook the pasta following its packet instructions, drain and cool under cold running water. Cook the peas separately following their packet instructions, then drain and cool.
  2. Heat the oven to 200°C / 180°C fan. Roast the pepper on a lined tray for about 20-25 minutes, turning halfway, until soft.
  3. While the pepper roasts, prepare the chicken. Cut the chicken into 1.5cm-thick strips. Cook in a non-stick pan over medium-high heat for about 8-12 minutes, turning often and adding a splash of water if it catches. Check the thickest part reaches 75°C for 30 seconds; cook longer if needed.
  4. Divide the roasted pepper in half. Blend one half with the yoghurt, lemon juice, basil, garlic, smoked paprika, black pepper and enough water to loosen.
  5. Toss the pasta with the remaining roasted pepper, cucumber, peas, spinach, chicken and dressing.
Tips & notes

Cool the pasta before adding the dressing so it stays creamy rather than absorbing it all.

High ProteinDairy freeOil FreeColourful
Why you’ll love it

A colourful protein-and-fibre lunch that breaks up the chicken-heavy days.

Ingredients
  • 120g salmon fillet
  • 120g cooked green or puy lentils, drained and rinsed
  • 100g plain cooked beetroot, diced
  • 60g cucumber, diced
  • 40g rocket
  • 1/2 orange, segmented
  • Fresh dill
  • Juice of 1/4 lemon
  • 1/2 tsp Dijon mustard
  • Black pepper
  • 1-2 tbsp water
Method
  1. Heat the oven to 200°C / 180°C fan. Put the salmon on a lined tray and bake for about 12-15 minutes, until opaque throughout and the thickest part flakes easily with a fork. Thicker fillets may need longer.
  2. Mix any juice from the orange with the lemon juice, Dijon, black pepper and a splash of water.
  3. Toss the lentils, beetroot, cucumber, rocket, orange segments and dill with the dressing.
  4. Flake the salmon over the salad and finish with extra dill and black pepper.
Tips & notes

Cook the salmon just until it flakes; overcooking is the fastest way to make this salad feel dry.
